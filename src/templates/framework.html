Framework exists to challenge a premise that the laptop industry spent twenty years making feel inevitable: that consumer laptops must be sealed, irreparable, and obsolete on the manufacturer's schedule. The Framework Laptop 13 and 16 are a counter-argument to every glued chassis and soldered RAM slot the industry has shipped in the past decade. And in 2026, with memory prices surging and repair costs climbing everywhere, that counter-argument has never felt more timely.

PCMag calls the Framework Laptop 16 "the most versatile and future-proof desktop-replacement laptop" available. PCWorld names the Framework 13 its pick for the best repairable laptop. The Framework CEO, meanwhile, has issued a stark warning: rising RAM and SSD prices through 2026 are making repairability not just a philosophical preference but a financial necessity.

What You Can Actually Replace

The Framework Laptop is not modular in a theoretical sense — it is modular in a practical sense. Users can replace the RAM with standard DDR5 modules. The SSD is a standard NVMe M.2 slot. The battery is secured with screws, not glue. The keyboard pulls out without specialized tools. Even the mainboard — containing the motherboard, processor, and fans — can be swapped out entirely when a new processor generation releases, turning a 2024 Framework into a 2026-spec machine for the cost of a mainboard upgrade rather than an entirely new laptop.

The expansion card system addresses the port problem that plagues thin laptops. Choose from USB-C, USB-A, HDMI, DisplayPort, MicroSD, ethernet, and storage expansion cards. Drop them into the four slots and reconfigure as your needs change. Traveling and need USB-A for a hotel alarm clock? Swap a card before you leave.

"Personal computing as we know it is dead — unless we build hardware that lasts. The RAM crisis makes that argument louder than it has ever been." — Framework CEO

The Repairability Argument in 2026

The timing of Framework's rise is not accidental. The global RAM shortage has sent memory prices up 50–300% depending on the tier. A sealed laptop with soldered RAM that degrades or fails is now substantially more expensive to deal with — either through repair (if even possible) or replacement. Framework users can buy a RAM upgrade kit and install it themselves in ten minutes. In a market where 16GB RAM kits have gone from 0 to 40+, the ability to choose when and how much to spend on memory upgrades is genuinely valuable.

The right-to-repair movement has also gained legislative traction, with several states passing repair-friendly laws in 2025. Framework was ahead of this wave — not as a compliance strategy, but as a founding philosophy. The brand has built a community of users who treat their laptops as long-term investments rather than disposable devices.

The Performance Reality Check

Framework is not for everyone. The AMD Ryzen AI 300-powered Framework 13 handles everyday productivity, development work, and light creative tasks well. It is not a gaming laptop — integrated graphics are the only option, and they handle indie games and light titles at modest settings. Battery life is decent but not class-leading. The chassis, while logically designed, is thicker and heavier than premium ultrabooks with equivalent components.
